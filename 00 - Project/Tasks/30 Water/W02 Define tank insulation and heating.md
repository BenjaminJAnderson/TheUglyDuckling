# W02 Define tank insulation and heating

## Outcome

A winter protection method is chosen for the tanks and pipework, with its power demand understood.

## Done when

- [ ] Tank dimensions and material recorded
- [ ] Insulation method chosen
- [ ] 12 V thermal heating mats or heater pads compared and selected
- [ ] Thermostat and temperature sensor specified
- [ ] Power budget added to [[Electrics Overview]]
- [ ] Low-point drain and winterising procedure documented
- [ ] [[Water]] and [[Decisions]] updated

## Notes

- The selected tanks are Autocraft 75 L fresh (300 × 1000 × 225 mm) and 60 L waste (350 × 800 × 250 mm).
- The 60 L waste tank is longer and narrower than the previous 45 L tank; check that insulation and heater pads fit around its brackets, inlets and service hatch.
- Research thermostatically controlled 12 V thermal heating mats or pads, including adhesive or strap fixing, frost protection, power draw and thermostat options.
- Add the heating load to the power budget and check it against the solar and battery capacity.
- The fresh tank sits behind the exhaust box; check heat gain as well as frost protection.
- Insulate the tanks, fittings and pipework; keep the drain taps accessible.
- Do not add antifreeze to the fresh-water tank.
- Keep the grey-tank drain valve accessible.
