# Fullriver DC115-12 AGM Deep-Cycle Leisure Battery

**Category:** Leisure / Auxiliary Battery  
**Manufacturer:** Fullriver  
**Model:** DC115-12 / DC115-12A  
**Battery chemistry:** AGM (Absorbent Glass Mat) lead-acid  
**Nominal voltage:** 12V  
**Capacity:** 115Ah @ 20-hour rate  
**Battery type:** Deep-cycle

## Purpose

The Fullriver DC115-12 is a **12V deep-cycle AGM battery** designed for repeated charging and discharging rather than the short bursts of very high current expected from a normal starter battery.

For your ambulance conversion, it is intended to form part of the **leisure battery bank**, storing energy generated by:

- Solar panels
    
- Alternator/DC-DC charger
    
- Sterling Pro Charge Ultra mains charger
    

It then supplies:

- 12V appliances
    
- DC distribution
    
- Inverter
    
- Lighting
    
- Pumps
    
- Fridge
    
- Other auxiliary equipment
    

Fullriver specifically markets the DC series for leisure, marine, renewable-energy and backup-power applications. ([fullriver.com](https://fullriver.com/product/DC115-12A.html "Batteries"))

## Capacity

The headline capacity is **115Ah**, measured at the 20-hour discharge rate.

```text
12V × 115Ah = 1,380Wh
```

So the battery contains approximately **1.38kWh of nominal energy**.

However, this is where AGM batteries differ significantly from lithium.

For good longevity, you generally don't want to routinely extract the entire 1,380Wh.

If we use a rough 50% depth-of-discharge design target:

```text
115Ah × 50% ≈ 57.5Ah

12V × 57.5Ah ≈ 690Wh
```

So I would think of this battery as providing roughly **0.7kWh of routinely usable energy**, rather than 1.38kWh.

---

## An Important Specification: Capacity Depends on Discharge Rate

The DC115-12 demonstrates an important characteristic of lead-acid batteries:

|Discharge rate|Rated capacity|
|---|--:|
|C100|128Ah|
|C20|**115Ah**|
|C5|91Ah|

This means the faster you discharge it, the less energy you can actually extract.

This becomes particularly relevant to your **2,000W Xantrex inverter**.

---

## Running The Xantrex PROwatt SW 2000i

At 2,000W:

```text
2,000W ÷ 12V ≈ 167A
```

After accounting for inverter losses, the battery could be supplying roughly **180A**.

That's an extremely heavy load for a 115Ah AGM battery.

The battery is capable of enormous short-duration currents — Fullriver specifies up to **1,100A for 5 seconds** — but that doesn't mean it is suitable for continuously powering a 2kW load. ([fullriver.com](https://fullriver.com/upload/article/20260402/69cdb744b6469.pdf "DC115-12A DATA SHEET"))

The problem is primarily:

- Voltage sag
    
- Reduced effective capacity
    
- Heat
    
- Rapid battery depletion
    
- Reduced cycle life
    

So:

> **The DC115-12 can provide the current, but a single battery is not an appropriate energy source for sustained 2kW inverter operation.**

## Charging

This battery has a particularly important specification for your build:

### Maximum charge current: 30A

Fullriver specifies **30A maximum charging current** for the DC115-12A. ([fullriver.com](https://fullriver.com/upload/article/20260402/69cdb744b6469.pdf "DC115-12A DATA SHEET"))

This has major implications for your planned charging system.

You have a:

**Sterling Pro Charge Ultra 12V-60A**

If you connected a **single DC115-12** to the charger and allowed it to deliver the full 60A:

```text
60A > 30A maximum battery charging current
```

That would exceed Fullriver's specified maximum.

### Two identical DC115-12 batteries in parallel

With two identical batteries:

```text
115Ah + 115Ah = 230Ah
```

and the charging current is shared between them:

```text
60A ÷ 2 = 30A per battery
```

That puts the Sterling's maximum 60A output **exactly at the specified 30A maximum per battery**.

This is a very important reason why, if you use this battery, I would strongly favour **two identical DC115-12 batteries rather than mixing the Fullriver with the Lucas.**

## Your Lucas + Fullriver Combination

You previously listed:

- Lucas LSLC125-12 — 125Ah AGM
    
- Fullriver DC115-12 — 115Ah AGM
    

I would **not recommend connecting these two batteries directly in parallel as your permanent leisure bank**.

Even though they're both:

- 12V
    
- AGM
    
- Deep-cycle
    
- Similar capacity
    

they are still different batteries.

Ideally a parallel battery bank should use batteries with the same:

- Manufacturer
    
- Model
    
- Capacity
    
- Age
    
- Condition
    
- Charging characteristics
    

### Better arrangement

If you wanted ~230Ah of AGM capacity:

```text
Fullriver DC115-12
       +
Fullriver DC115-12
       ↓
230Ah @ 12V
```

Alternatively, two identical Lucas batteries.

I would **not buy another battery until you've decided which battery you're committing to.**

## Your Solar System

You've chosen:

**4 × 200W = 800W solar**

Theoretical maximum charging current around battery charging voltage:

```text
800W ÷ 14V ≈ 57A
```

That's roughly comparable to your Sterling's 60A output.

For **one 115Ah battery**, that could theoretically represent almost:

```text
57A ÷ 115Ah ≈ 0.50C
```

which is well above the Fullriver's specified **30A maximum charging current**.

With **two DC115-12 batteries**:

```text
230Ah bank
57A ÷ 230Ah ≈ 0.25C
```

and approximately 28.5A would reach each battery if current shared equally.

That's much better aligned with the battery's specifications.

**This is another strong argument for a 230Ah matched bank if you stick with these batteries.**

## Weight

One of the biggest disadvantages is weight.

A single battery:

**32.7kg**

Two:

```text
32.7kg × 2 = 65.4kg
```

So a 230Ah AGM bank would weigh approximately **65kg** before cables, mounting and fusing.

That's a significant amount of weight in an ambulance conversion.

For comparison, a similarly sized LiFePO4 bank can provide substantially more usable energy for considerably less weight.

## AGM Characteristics

AGM means **Absorbent Glass Mat**.

The electrolyte is held within a fibreglass separator rather than freely sloshing around inside the battery.

The DC series uses high-purity lead construction and is designed to be resistant to vibration and repeated cycling. Fullriver also describes the series as maintenance-free. ([Tayna](https://www.tayna.co.uk/leisure-batteries/fullriver/dc115-12/ "DC115-12 FullRiver DC Series Deep Cycle AGM Leisure Battery 115Ah"))

## Ambulance Build Assessment

### As a single battery

**❌ I would not use this as the sole leisure battery for your planned system.**

The combination of:

- 800W solar
    
- 60A mains charger
    
- 2,000W inverter
    

is simply too substantial for a single 115Ah AGM.

### Two DC115-12 batteries

**👍 Much more sensible.**

You'd have:

```text
230Ah nominal
≈2.76kWh nominal energy
≈1.38kWh at 50% DoD
```

- 60A Sterling charger → 30A per battery
    
- ~57A maximum solar → ~28.5A per battery

## One Thing I Would Reconsider

Looking at **your entire electrical system rather than this battery in isolation**, I think the battery choice deserves another discussion.

Your system is becoming quite large:

```text
                    800W SOLAR
                        │
                        ▼
                   60A MPPT
                        │
                        │
230V ──► 60A CHARGER ──┼──► BATTERY BANK
                        │
Alternator ─► DC-DC ────┘
                              │
                              ▼
                       2000W INVERTER
```

A **230Ah AGM bank** can work, but you're going to be carrying ~65kg of batteries while only having around **1.4kWh of sensible usable energy**.

Before you purchase the final battery bank, I think it would be worth comparing this against a **~200–300Ah LiFePO4 bank**. Given the amount of solar and the 2kW inverter you've chosen, lithium may make considerably more sense for the ambulance.
