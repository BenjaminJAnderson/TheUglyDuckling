# Ventilation & Moisture

## Overview

Ventilation is critical in a sealed ambulance. Cooking, showering, drying gear and the separating toilet all produce moisture, odour and condensation. The kitchen and shower need extract ventilation, the toilet compartment needs an air path, and the living space needs replacement air.

## Requirements

- Kitchen extractor for cooking fumes, grease and moisture.
- Shower extractor for steam, with a **new roof vent**.
- Compartment ventilation for toilet odour and moisture; no dedicated toilet vent.
- Passive or controlled inlet air.
- A ducted heat supply from the diesel heater to the shower for drying.
- The ability to switch the heater between the living space and the shower.
- Humidity and temperature monitoring.
- Insulation and vapour control to reduce condensation.
- External vent terminations protected from rain and insects.
- CO alarm for the diesel heater.

## Current design

### Kitchen

- Extractor hood or fan above/beside the induction hob.
- Grease filter.
- Duct to outside, or a recirculating charcoal filter if ducting is impractical.

### Shower

- **New 12 V extractor fan**; do not reuse the existing black roof extractor.
- Humidity sensor or timer, plus a manual boost switch.
- Duct to a **new roof vent** with a cowl, insect mesh and backdraught shutter.
- Insulated duct to reduce condensation.
- Passive air inlet so the extractor can move air.
- Wipe down and squeegee the compartment after use.

### Drying room and diesel heater

The Eberspächer Airtronic D4 is already installed. See [[Diesel Heater]].

- **89 mm hot-air duct** into the shower.
- A **changeover damper or vent switch** so the heated air can be directed to either the living space or the shower.
- Keep the main heater outlet open; do not dead-end the heater when the shower branch is closed.
- **76 mm return air** drawn from the dry living space, not from the shower.
- **24 mm combustion air and exhaust** terminate outside.
- Eberspächer 802 digital controller.
- High-temperature, closable diffuser in the shower.
- High-temperature ducting, clamps and a heat shield.
- CO alarm.

### Toilet

- **No dedicated toilet vent.** The selected Boxio XL MAX+ has no integrated fan or direct vent port.
- Odour control relies on the Boxio plug/odour trap, hemp litter and regular emptying.
- The toilet remains portable and moves out of the shower compartment when showering.
- The shower/room extractor provides compartment ventilation; run it after toilet use.
- Do not install a lower-back-wall vent, flexible hose, docking hood, carbon filter or 12 V toilet fan unless the design changes.
- Keep the lid closed when the toilet is not in use.

### Living space

- Roof vent/skylight or wall vents.
- A path for replacement air when extractors run.
- Avoid sealing the van completely.
- Consider a humidity sensor and a small dehumidifier for winter.

### Drying sequence

1. Squeegee the walls and shower tray.
2. Run the extractor for 10–15 minutes.
3. Direct heater air to the shower, with a vent open and a small circulation fan if needed.
4. Finish with a short extract to remove the moist air.

Heat alone is not enough: moisture needs a path out of the compartment.

## Condensation and insulation

- Insulate walls, ceiling and floor.
- Reduce thermal bridges.
- Use a vapour-control layer where appropriate.
- Keep water pipes and tanks insulated.
- Vent moisture at the source.
- Wipe down wet surfaces after showering.
- Check for leaks and damp regularly.
- The PVC cladding and plywood backing are vapour-tight; moisture can form behind them if the wall is cold. Insulate behind the plywood, seal penetrations and seal the plywood edges.
